Acts 16 · Philippi · Midnight

How can I be saved?

It is the most important question you will ever ask. Long ago, a terrified man asked it in the dark, and the answer he received turned his midnight into morning. That same answer is offered to you.

Begin at midnight
  1. 25But at midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them.
  2. 26Suddenly there was a great earthquake, so that the foundations of the prison were shaken; and immediately all the doors were opened and everyone’s chains were loosed.
  3. 27And the keeper of the prison, awaking from sleep and seeing the prison doors open, supposing the prisoners had fled, drew his sword and was about to kill himself.
  4. 28But Paul called with a loud voice, saying, “Do yourself no harm, for we are all here.”
  5. 29Then he called for a light, ran in, and fell down trembling before Paul and Silas. 30And he brought them out and said,

“Sirs, what must I do to be saved?”

Moments earlier, this man had been ready to take his own life. Now he stood trembling on the edge of eternity, and he knew he was not ready to meet God. He was not asking for a philosophy, a self-help plan, or a more comfortable life. He wanted to know how a guilty man could escape the judgment he deserved.

You may never have felt the ground shake beneath you. But every one of us will one day stand before God. The question is not whether you will meet Him, but how.

Paul’s answer is coming. First, though, we need to ask a question of our own.

Saved from what?

  1. I

    God is holy.

    The God who made you is not a distant force or an indulgent grandfather. He is infinitely pure, just, and good, and He made you by Him and for Him (Col. 1:16) to glorify Him and to enjoy Him forever.

    “Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of His glory!” Isaiah 6:3
  2. II

    We have sinned.

    “Sin is any want of conformity unto, or transgression of, the law of God” (Shorter Catechism 14). It is not only what we do, but what we are: hearts turned away from the God who made us.

    “For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.” Romans 3:23
  3. III

    Judgment is certain.

    A judge who shrugged at evil would not be good. Because God is just, every sin will be answered for, and the penalty is more serious than most of us dare to imagine.

    “It is appointed for men to die once, but after this the judgment.” Hebrews 9:27

Look into the mirror

Most of us think we are basically good people. But Jesus taught that God’s law reaches past our actions and into our hearts (James 1:23-25). Turn each card to see how He explained it.

Measured by that standard, none of us is innocent. And this is what we need to be saved from: not merely from punishment, but from sin itself, and from the righteous anger of a holy God against it (Rom. 1:18; 6:23). That is why, before Jesus was born, the angel said, “You shall call His name JESUS, for He will save His people from their sins” (Matt. 1:21).

Can I save myself?

Our instinct is to fix it ourselves: to balance the scales, to make up for what we have done, to climb back up to God. But every ladder we build falls short.

  • Trying harder
  • Being a good person
  • Growing up in a Christian home
  • Being baptized
  • Joining a church
  • Feeling sincere
  • Repeating a prayer

Some of these are precious gifts. Baptism is a sign and seal God has appointed for His people, and membership in Christ’s church is a great privilege; we treasure both. But no outward thing can save a person whose heart remains far from God. “All our righteousnesses are like filthy rags” (Isa. 64:6).

The deeper problem is this: the Bible does not describe us as sick and needing a little help, but as “dead in trespasses and sins” (Eph. 2:1). A dead man cannot climb. He cannot even want to. He needs to be raised.

If our salvation depended on us, the story would end here, in the dark.

Ephesians 2:4-5

But God

…who is rich in mercy, because of His great love with which He loved us, even when we were dead in trespasses, made us alive together with Christ (by grace you have been saved).

What we could never do, God has done. He did not wait for us to climb up to Him. He came down.

“For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life.” John 3:16

The Savior

Salvation is not a program or a principle. It is a Person. “Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners” (1 Tim. 1:15), and there is no other.

  1. He came.

    The eternal Son of God, equal with the Father, took to Himself a true human body and soul. “The Word became flesh and dwelt among us” (John 1:14).

  2. He obeyed.

    He lived the life we have not lived. He was “in all points tempted as we are, yet without sin” (Heb. 4:15), keeping God’s law perfectly, from the heart.

  3. He died.

    On the cross He bore the wrath of God against sin, not for anything He had done, but for His people. “Christ also suffered once for sins, the just for the unjust, that He might bring us to God” (1 Pet. 3:18).

  4. He rose.

    On the third day He rose bodily from the grave, proving that His sacrifice was accepted (1 Cor. 15:3-4). He “was delivered up because of our offenses, and was raised because of our justification” (Rom. 4:25).

Today He reigns at the right hand of the Father, and “there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved” (Acts 4:12).

“For He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God in Him.” 2 Corinthians 5:21

“It is finished!” John 19:30

Here is the wonder of the gospel. On the cross, God treated His Son as though He had lived our life, so that He might treat everyone who trusts in Him as though they had lived His. Our sin was counted to Christ, and He paid for it in full. His perfect righteousness is counted to us as a free gift.

The Bible calls this justification: “an act of God’s free grace, wherein he pardoneth all our sins, and accepteth us as righteous in his sight, only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us, and received by faith alone” (Shorter Catechism 33).

“Though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be as white as snow.” Isaiah 1:18

“Sirs, what must I do to be saved?”

“Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved.”

Acts 16:31

Not “do better.” Not “earn it.” Not “wait until you are worthy.” Believe on Him. Salvation is not a reward for the good; it is a gift for the guilty. “For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, lest anyone should boast” (Eph. 2:8-9).

Faith

“Faith in Jesus Christ is a saving grace, whereby we receive and rest upon him alone for salvation, as he is offered to us in the gospel.”

Shorter Catechism 86

Faith is more than knowing facts about Jesus. It is trusting Him: letting go of every other hope and resting your whole weight on Him, the way a drowning man rests on the arms that pull him from the water.

from sinto God

Repentance

“Repentance unto life is a saving grace, whereby a sinner, out of a true sense of his sin, and apprehension of the mercy of God in Christ, doth, with grief and hatred of his sin, turn from it unto God, with full purpose of, and endeavour after, new obedience.”

Shorter Catechism 87

Repentance is not cleaning yourself up before you come to Christ. It is coming to Him as you are and turning your back on sin because you have seen His mercy.

Faith and repentance are not two rungs on a ladder. They are two sides of the same turning: away from sin and toward Christ. “Repent, and believe in the gospel” (Mark 1:15). Both are gifts that God works in us by His Spirit (John 3:3-8; Acts 11:18), and both are commanded of all who hear the gospel.

He is willing.

Perhaps you are wondering whether someone like you could ever be welcomed. Listen to the Savior Himself.

  • “Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.” Matthew 11:28
  • “The one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out.” John 6:37
  • “Whoever desires, let him take the water of life freely.” Revelation 22:17

Call on Him today.

There are no magic words, and no prayer saves by its own power. But the Lord hears every sinner who calls on Him in faith: “Whoever calls on the name of the LORD shall be saved” (Rom. 10:13). You might begin where one man did in Jesus’ parable, standing far off, unable even to lift his eyes:

“God, be merciful to me a sinner!” Luke 18:13

Jesus said that man went home justified (Luke 18:14). Do not wait for a better moment. “Behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation” (2 Cor. 6:2).

Acts 16:33-34

From midnight to morning

The jailer’s night did not end in despair. That same hour he washed the wounds of the men he had locked away, and he and his household were baptized. Then he brought them into his house and set food before them, “and he rejoiced, having believed in God with all his household.”

If you have come to Christ, or if you want to know more, here is where to go next. We would love to walk with you.

  • Read

    Begin with the Gospel of John. It was written “that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in His name” (John 20:31).

  • Pray

    Speak to God honestly, every day. Confess your sin, ask for mercy, and thank Him for His Son. He invites you to come boldly to His throne of grace (Heb. 4:16).

  • Worship

    Christ gathers His people as a family. Join us on the Lord’s Day to hear His Word preached and to sing His praise.
